Lesson 0020

Semi-additive measures, statistics and quick measures

ModelAbout 15 minAfter this lesson you can write a closing-balance measure and use basic statistics and quick measures correctly.

Why this matters for the exam

Balances and stock levels show up in practice questions as a "why is my monthly total too big" puzzle, and quick measures appear as settings questions (how many periods before and after). Both are short facts that are easy to lock in, and they sit in the Model the data domain.

Recall first

Which function returns the same selected dates one year earlier?

SAMEPERIODLASTYEAR (or DATEADD with -1 YEAR). PREVIOUSYEAR returns the whole prior year.

Core idea

A semi-additive value (account balance, inventory on hand, headcount) can be summed across accounts or products but not across time. Adding Monday's and Tuesday's stock double counts the same boxes. Pick one date instead: the last one in the period.

NeedPattern
Closing valueCALCULATE(SUM(Inv[Qty]), LASTDATE('Date'[Date]))
Closing value when the last calendar day may have no snapshotLASTNONBLANK('Date'[Date], [Qty]) inside CALCULATE
Opening valueFIRSTDATE or FIRSTNONBLANK
Accounts on the last day onlyCALCULATE(DISTINCTCOUNT(Bal[AccountID]), LASTDATE('Date'[Date]))

LASTDATE; more patterns at DAX Guide.Basic statistics you should recognise: SUM, AVERAGE, MEDIAN, MIN, MAX, COUNT, DISTINCTCOUNT, STDEV.P/S, PERCENTILE.INC/EXC and the X iterator versions.DAX overview. AVERAGE ignores blanks and MEDIAN is the middle value.

Quick measures (right-click a table or field, New quick measure) generate DAX for common patterns: rolling average, year-to-date, year-over-year change, running total, star rating. For a rolling average of 7 days including today enter 6 periods before and 0 after.Quick measures. Time-based quick measures need the date column of the marked date table or an auto date/time hierarchy; a bare many-side date column is rejected.

Practical example

Business context: a warehouse loads daily inventory snapshots. A monthly matrix must show month-end stock, but the current measure (SUM of Units) shows about thirty times too much.

Stock on Hand =
CALCULATE (
    SUM ( Inventory[Units] ),
    LASTNONBLANK ( 'Date'[Date], CALCULATE ( SUM ( Inventory[Units] ) ) )
)

Why it works: in each month, LASTNONBLANK finds the latest date that actually has a snapshot, and CALCULATE sums units for that single date only. Across products it still adds up, which is what semi-additive means. At the grand total the last date of the whole range is used, so the total is the latest stock, not a sum of months.

Traps

Wrong belief: SUM across days is fine for inventory.
Correct rule: It overstates stock. Take the last (or first) date in the period.

Wrong belief: COUNTROWS of headcount snapshots across months gives headcount.
Correct rule: It counts snapshot rows. Count distinct employees on the last date.

Wrong belief: A 7-day average including today needs 7 periods before.
Correct rule: Today counts as one of the 7, so use 6 before and 0 after.
