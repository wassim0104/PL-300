Lesson 0012

Design a star schema: facts, dimensions and keys

Model the dataAbout 18 minSkill: sort columns into facts and dimensions and pick keys

After this lesson you can sort source columns into fact and dimension tables and choose a relationship key for each dimension.

Why this matters for the exam

Model the data is a major exam domain, and star schema is its foundation: relationships, DAX and performance all assume it. Practice questions ask you to sort columns, flatten chains and pick keys. Get this right and later modelling lessons become easy.

Recall first

What does Enable load off do, and what does it not do?

Show answer

It makes the query connection-only, so it is not in the model. Clearing Include in report refresh does not remove the table.

Core idea

Fact tables store events (sales lines, attendance): numeric columns to aggregate plus foreign keys, many rows, growing over time. Dimension tables describe things (Customer, Product, Date, Teacher): a unique key plus descriptive attributes used to filter and group. Facts in the middle with dimensions around them is a star schema, Microsoft’s recommended design for performance and simple DAX. Understand star schema.Glossary: Star schema, Fact table, Dimension table.

Snowflake chains (Product, Subcategory, Category) are usually flattened into one dimension with merges (lesson 0008). For header/detail facts, merge the header attributes into the line-level fact so visuals do not cross two fact tables. Attributes fixed for a dimension member, such as teacher name or class period, belong on the dimension, not repeated on the fact.

Keys must be unique and non-blank in the dimension. Prefer a compact integer surrogate key; do not concatenate text keys, which raises cardinality and size. To build a unique dimension, keep Country, drop City, then Remove Duplicates on Country. Sometimes separate semantic models are needed for scope or security: analysts who must not see the balance sheet need their own model, or RLS/OLS. Model relationships.

Practical example

A school exports one flat table: StudentID, StudentName, ClassID, Period, TeacherName, Date, Present.

  1. Fact: events are attendance rows, so FactAttendance(StudentKey, ClassKey, DateKey, Present).
  2. Dimensions: DimStudent, DimClass(ClassKey, Period, TeacherName), DimDate.
  3. In Power Query, Reference the flat query for each dimension, keep its columns, Remove Duplicates on its key.
DimClass = Table.Distinct(
    Table.SelectColumns(Flat, {"ClassID", "Period", "TeacherName"}), {"ClassID"})

Teacher and period are constant per class, so they live once on the dimension instead of on every attendance row. Filters on any dimension reach the fact through one-to-many relationships (next lesson).

Traps

Wrong belief: “descriptive columns can stay on the fact.” Correct rule: they repeat per row; move them to the dimension.

Wrong belief: “remove duplicates on City to get countries.” Correct rule: drop City, then de-duplicate on Country.

Wrong belief: “relate two fact tables directly.” Correct rule: connect them through shared dimensions.
